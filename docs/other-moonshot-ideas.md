# Other Moonshot-Worthy Ideas

These projects share OpenAction's core belief: difficult real-world information should become verifiable, useful action—not another black-box AI answer.

## Public Data Compiler

An open system that turns public PDFs, circulars, spreadsheets, and portals into structured, versioned data products. Each output retains its source snapshot, extraction history, and change feed. Start with one valuable domain, then become infrastructure for journalists, civic groups, founders, and developers.

## Opportunity Graph

A global map of grants, tenders, jobs, scholarships, fellowships, accelerators, and public schemes. Users maintain a private profile; the graph explains *why* an opportunity may fit and links straight to the authoritative application path.

## Agentic Business Autopilot

A cautious AI operator for small businesses. It connects only to authorised business systems, prepares bounded work—lead follow-ups, order summaries, deadline reminders, daily reports—and always asks before high-impact actions. The product is an operational teammate, not a chatbot.

## Local Truth Network

A community-owned, evidence-backed map of local services, prices, procedures, availability, and public issues. It rewards current, checkable contributions and shows uncertainty, creating a useful alternative to stale SEO pages and anonymous recommendations.

## GitHub for Real-World Operations

Version, review, test, and audit standard operating procedures for clinics, factories, schools, agencies, and field teams. AI can draft a procedure, but people approve it; teams retain change history, checklists, incident links, and evidence of execution.

## Personal Institution

A private, local-first life operating system that maintains a person's documents, commitments, skills, learning history, opportunities, and important deadlines across decades. It should be portable, inspectable, and user-owned rather than an advertising profile.

## India's Machine-Readable Rulebook

An evidence-first system that represents selected Indian rules, requirements, and effective dates as computable logic. It never substitutes for professional advice; instead, it explains applicable official material, keeps the version history, and routes high-risk cases to a human expert.

## Why these belong together

All seven projects can share a common substrate: source registry, immutable snapshots, extractors, structured facts, version comparison, explainable rules, and human review. OpenAction Network is the practical first expression of that substrate.
